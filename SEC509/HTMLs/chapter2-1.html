2.1 Personal and Corporate Cyber Hygiene

What is Cyber Hygiene?

Cyber hygiene is a reference to the practices and steps that users of computers and other devices take to maintain system health and improve online security. These practices are often part of a routine to ensure the safety of identity and other details that could be stolen or corrupted.

Much like physical hygiene, cyber hygiene is not a one-time event but a continuous process that requires regular attention and maintenance.

Personal Cyber Hygiene

On an individual level, cyber hygiene focuses on protecting personal data and preventing common attacks like phishing or identity theft.

Core Habits for Security

  • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Using at least two different methods of authentication whenever possible. This remains the single most effective defense against credential theft.
  • Regular Software Updates: Promptly installing security patches for operating systems, browsers, and applications to close vulnerabilities.
  • Robust Password Management: Moving away from predictable passwords to complex, unique passphrases stored in a secure password manager.
  • Device Security: Utilizing disk encryption, screen locks, and remote wipe capabilities for mobile devices.
Tip: Automate where possible. Setting your system to auto-update and using an authenticator app rather than SMS can significantly boost your personal security posture.

Corporate Cyber Hygiene

In a business context, cyber hygiene expands from individual habits to organizational policies and infrastructure management.

The Corporate Framework

  • Asset Management: Knowing every device and software service running on the corporate network. Shadow IT is a major risk factor.
  • Privilege Management: Following the Principle of Least Privilege—granting users only the access they need to perform their jobs.
  • Network Segmentation: Dividing the network into smaller segments to prevent "lateral movement" if an attacker gains entry.
  • Regular Backups: Ensuring critical data is backed up frequently and stored in an immutable or offline state to defend against ransomware.

Corporate Culture: Effective corporate hygiene is not just technical; it requires a culture where security is everyone's responsibility, from the CEO to the interns.

Conclusion

Maintaining high standards of cyber hygiene significantly reduces the "attack surface" of both individuals and organizations. While it cannot prevent 100% of advanced threats, it eliminates the vast majority of low-effort, opportunistic attacks that cause the most common security breaches.
